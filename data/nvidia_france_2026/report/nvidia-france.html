NVIDIA in France

Shareholder, supplier, research partner and political ally: how the American chipmaker wove itself into French innovation

When Jensen Huang stepped down as NVIDIA's chief executive on 12 November 2026, he left a company whose reach into France goes far beyond selling the chips that train AI models. In three years NVIDIA has become a shareholder in more than a dozen French and French-founded companies, agreed to buy Hugging Face, and co-founded a giant AI campus outside Paris with the French state bank. Its graphics processors run the country's public supercomputers, its researchers co-sign papers with CNRS and Inria, and its chief executive has become a regular on stage beside Emmanuel Macron and Mistral AI's Arthur Mensch.

That footprint has never been assembled in one place. We pieced it together from the company register, EU research and French publication databases, the lobbying and public-procurement registers, French Tech Journal's own reporting and company announcements. One thing runs through all of it: NVIDIA almost never says how much money it puts into any French company, lab or programme.

Leadership change

Huang resigned as CEO and left the board with immediate effect on 12 November, according to NVIDIA's Form 8-K filing. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Most of the French announcements below were fronted by Huang personally.

The footprint

Where NVIDIA shows up in France

Most of NVIDIA's French presence is concentrated around Paris, where its investments, Mistral's data centres and the national supercomputers sit within an hour of each other. 1 · A French base

From modems to Biot: NVIDIA's quiet research office on the Riviera

NVIDIA runs two companies in France. Its sales arm is a branch of the British subsidiary NVIDIA Ltd, with an office at La Défense in Courbevoie. Its research arm is NVIDIA Développement France, a company of 50 to 99 people in Biot, inside the Sophia Antipolis technology park between Nice and Cannes.

The Biot office has had a turbulent history, and what it works on today is not public.

What is Sophia Antipolis? Europe's best-known science park

Sophia Antipolis is a technology park founded in 1969 on the plateau above Antibes, spread across Biot, Valbonne, Mougins and neighbouring towns. It hosts research centres of Inria, the engineering school EURECOM and the CNRS alongside chip and telecom companies such as Arm, NXP and Analog Devices. It is one of the few places in France with a deep pool of semiconductor and wireless engineers, which is what first drew NVIDIA there.

2011

Arrival through Icera

NVIDIA bought Icera, a British designer of mobile-phone modems, and with it a Sophia Antipolis R&D team of about 60 people. French engineers there are named on NVIDIA patents filed in 2010–2012 for communication systems and remote graphics. Patents

2015

The modem exit

When NVIDIA abandoned the modem business in 2015, it cut about 500 jobs worldwide, including roughly 123 in Sophia Antipolis, and announced the closure of the site. Press

2016 to today

A second start in Biot

The company register shows NVIDIA Développement France opened a new site at 400 avenue Roumanille in Biot on 25 March 2016. In 2024 it employed 50–99 people and reported revenue of €27.1M and net income of €2.1M, the profile of a subsidiary billing its parent for research work. Company register

Open question for NVIDIA: what does the Biot team work on, and how many people does NVIDIA employ in France? Its annual accounts do not say, and we found no recent job listings for the site. The register lists the business as research and development in physical and natural sciences.

2 · The closest partner

Mistral AI: shareholder, supplier, co-developer

No French company is more entangled with NVIDIA than Mistral AI. NVIDIA has invested in three of its funding rounds, sells it the chips for its data centres, co-builds models with it, and sits beside it in the joint venture planning France's largest AI campus. Mistral's leadership is open about the dependence.

"Owning the chips may come, I think it should come at some point, but for now we are relying on Nvidia."Arthur Mensch, Mistral AI CEO, as reported by French Tech Journal, May 2026

The shareholder

NVIDIA joined Mistral's €600M Series B in June 2024, returned for the €1.7bn Series C led by ASML in September 2025, and is reported as a participant in the €3bn Series D led by Samsung in September 2026. Its stake and cheque sizes have never been disclosed. Navigator

The compute supplier

At VivaTech in June 2025, with Emmanuel Macron on stage, NVIDIA and Mistral announced Mistral Compute, a French AI cloud built on 18,000 Grace Blackwell superchips. The first site, at Bruyères-le-Châtel in Essonne, was running by June 2026. Mistral is now aiming for 1 GW of capacity by 2030. Press

The co-developer

The two companies built Mistral NeMo in 2024 on NVIDIA's DGX Cloud and tuned the Mistral 3 family for NVIDIA hardware in 2025. In March 2026 Mistral became a founding member of the Nemotron Coalition, NVIDIA's group of open-model builders. Mistral and NVIDIA alone are co-developing the base model for the coalition's Nemotron 4 family. Mistral, FTJ

The industrial-AI bet

On 21 October 2026, Mistral and NVIDIA extended the partnership to engineering simulation. Mistral Large 4 and Mistral's physics-modelling pipelines will run on NVIDIA's CUDA-X libraries and Warp to connect simulation, model training and design in a closed loop. The models will also plug into NVIDIA Omniverse for 3D scene generation. The targets are aerospace, automotive and semiconductors. Mistral

Campus IA: the joint venture outside Paris NVIDIA, MGX, Bpifrance and Mistral AI

At the Choose France summit in May 2025, NVIDIA, Abu Dhabi's MGX fund, the state bank Bpifrance and Mistral AI announced a joint venture to build Europe's largest AI campus at Fouju in Seine-et-Marne, starting at 1.4 GW, with operations from 2028. In June 2026 Bpifrance widened the plan into a national network targeting 3 GW, with a second site to come. École Polytechnique is the academic partner and plans a teaching and research chair.

The price tag is unclear. Early reports cited about €8.5bn; French Tech Journal has since reported €50bn (November 2025) and €35bn (May 2026) for the full build-out. NVIDIA's equity share is not public, and Mensch has described Mistral's own stake as small. Press, FTJ

3 · The cheque book

From one deal to a portfolio

NVIDIA's French portfolio began quietly. In May 2023 its venture arm, NVentures, co-led a $55.4M round in Moon Surgical, a Paris maker of surgical robots. Three years later Huang stood in a hall off Place Vendôme and announced four French stakes in one evening.

In between, NVIDIA backed AI model makers, quantum and photonics hardware, voice AI and enterprise software. In every case the amount it put in is undisclosed.

"I was here one year ago, and at the time, NVIDIA's investment in Europe was zero… we've invested $8 billion, more than any VC."Jensen Huang, Paris, 13 October 2026, as reported by French Tech Journal
September 2026 · pending

Hugging Face

NVIDIA agreed to buy the French-founded open-source AI platform for about $12.93bn, nearly three times its 2023 valuation, with roughly $1bn in retention bonuses. Closing is expected in 2027 and needs regulatory approval. Pollen Robotics, a Bordeaux start-up that Hugging Face bought in 2025, comes with it. Press

13 October 2026

Doctolib and three AI start-ups

At a Paris reception, Huang announced a minority stake in Doctolib, France's medical-booking giant, and planned investments in Pigment, Entalpic and Genesis AI. That evening he met Macron at the Élysée. Navigator

AMI Labs

4 · The engine room

The chips under French AI

NVIDIA's biggest footprint in France is hardware that others buy. The country's public supercomputers, its sovereign-cloud ambitions and its largest corporate AI projects run largely on NVIDIA processors. Yet in 3.48 million French public-procurement records, NVIDIA never appears as the supplier: public buyers go through integrators such as Eviden, HPE and Dell.

Jean Zay, the public AI workhorse

France's main AI supercomputer for researchers and start-ups sits at the CNRS's IDRIS centre in Orsay. In March 2024 GENCI and the CNRS chose Eviden for a France 2030-funded extension that added 1,456 NVIDIA H100 chips to 416 A100s and 1,832 V100s, quadrupling its power to 125.9 petaflops. Project requests went from 72 in 2019 to 1,400 in 2024. About 150 companies have used it, including Valeo for some 30 autonomous-driving projects. CNRS

Opening the doors to start-ups

In November 2025 GENCI's AI Factory France announced Europe's first AI Factory tie-up with NVIDIA's start-up programmes. It went live at VivaTech in June 2026, giving eligible members access to Jean Zay, and NVIDIA joined its advisory committee. In November 2025 GENCI also unveiled Dalia, billed as Europe's first GB200 NVL72 system for open research. GENCI

5 · In the labs

Twenty years of research ties

Long before the AI boom, NVIDIA researchers were writing papers with French scientists. The earliest record in France's open archive, HAL, is a 2005 paper on computer arithmetic with researchers then at ENS Lyon. From 2009 to 2011 a Grenoble Inria team and NVIDIA engineers published "GigaVoxels" and voxel cone tracing, widely cited techniques for real-time lighting.

Today the ties run through four channels: co-written papers, EU research projects, university partnerships and hardware donations.

95 co-written papers

French institutions and NVIDIA staff co-signed 95 papers in HAL. CNRS appears on 75 and Inria on 53, followed by Paris-Saclay, Bordeaux, CEA, Centrale Lyon, INSA Rennes and Inserm. Topics range from graphics and supercomputing to medical imaging, 6G and climate models. HAL

Reims, the first CUDA university

In 2015 the University of Reims became France's first NVIDIA "CUDA Research Center" and opened a GPU application lab at its ROMEO supercomputing centre. From 2017 to 2020 NVIDIA, CEA and Bull were industrial partners in its supercomputing chair. NVIDIA's contribution is not disclosed. University

Hardware donations

About 295 French papers thank NVIDIA for donated graphics cards from its academic grant programme, peaking in 2018–2020. In October 2026 NVIDIA became a founding member and lead contributor of Fondation Inria's working group on open-source AI. Neither has a published value. HAL, Fondation Inria

A personal gift, not a corporate one

NVIDIA engineer Franck Diard, a Polytech Nice Sophia graduate, gave €400,000 over four years to the Université Côte d'Azur foundation, funding a supercomputer with NVIDIA chips. The money came from him, not from NVIDIA. University

6 · Corporate partners

Inside French industry

NVIDIA's pitch to European industry is that factories, cars and laboratories are the next market for AI. France's industrial champions have signed on one by one, usually in announcements timed to VivaTech or NVIDIA's own conferences.

Dassault Systèmes

In February 2026 the 3D-design leader and NVIDIA announced a long-term partnership to build an industrial AI platform around Dassault's "virtual twins". It runs both ways. NVIDIA adopts Dassault's systems-engineering software to design its own AI factories, and Dassault's cloud arm Outscale builds sovereign AI factories on NVIDIA hardware. The two also combine NVIDIA's BioNeMo with Dassault's BIOVIA software for molecule and materials research. Dassault Systèmes

Valeo, Stellantis and the self-driving push

On 21 October 2026 Valeo said it would develop and industrialise car computers on NVIDIA's Hyperion design for Level 4 autonomous driving. Days later, Wayve, Stellantis and NVIDIA joined the French government on bringing self-driving cars to French roads. Stellantis provides the vehicles, Wayve the driving software and NVIDIA the computing platform. The transport minister promised to adapt regulation; no launch date was given. Valeo, press

Schneider Electric

At GTC Paris in June 2025, the energy-management group announced reference designs with NVIDIA for AI-factory power and cooling, and a rack system for NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72. Schneider Electric

L'Oréal

The beauty group uses NVIDIA AI Enterprise for its CreAItech generative 3D content platform and its Noli marketplace, announced at VivaTech 2025. Press

The quantum trio

France's three leading quantum start-ups, Pasqal, Quandela and Alice & Bob, all connect their machines to NVIDIA's CUDA-Q software and NVQLink interconnect. Only Alice & Bob has NVIDIA as a shareholder. Company releases

Health, robotics and media

Owkin has used NVIDIA's Clara platform since 2019 and in 2026 began building its OwkinZero biology model with NVIDIA. Wandercraft's humanoid robot runs on NVIDIA's GR00T models. Omi, a Paris maker of AI product imagery, gets NVIDIA chips and engineers through an October 2026 partnership. Press

Telecoms and cloud: Orange Business and Bouygues Telecom have both launched AI services on NVIDIA infrastructure.

7 · Start-up programmes

Credits, chips and a seat at the table

Beyond equity, NVIDIA courts French start-ups through free programmes that offer discounts, training and computing credits, and that make NVIDIA's software the default.

NVIDIA Inception

NVIDIA says more than 700 French companies belong to its free start-up programme, out of more than 4,000 in Europe. Membership means support and discounts, not investment. NVIDIA's figure

AI Factory France

Since June 2026, Inception members doing open research with R&D in Europe can apply for time on Jean Zay and other public machines. Early users include Pleias and Ryax. GENCI

Scaleway and Sofinnova

Scaleway has offered Inception members credits on its H100 cluster since November 2023. Sofinnova Partners' life-science portfolio, including Bioptimus and Cure51, received DGX Cloud credits in June 2025. Press

9 · Influence and scrutiny

Lobbyists, regulators and open questions

An antitrust case still open

France's competition authority raided NVIDIA's Paris office in September 2023. In 2024 its president, Benoît Cœuré, told Reuters the company would be charged "if the investigation is fruitful". In July 2026 the authority said the inquiry was nearing its end; reported concerns include developers' dependence on NVIDIA's CUDA software and its investments in AI cloud providers. No charges or decision had been announced by 13 November 2026. Press, FTJ

Lobbying on the record

France's lobbying register shows the PR firm Brunswick lobbied MPs and the Economy ministry for NVIDIA in 2020–21 on its planned takeover of Arm. NVIDIA Ltd is listed among AmCham France's members. NVIDIA has never registered as a lobbyist in its own name. HATVP

Public money flows mostly toward NVIDIA's hardware rather than to the company itself. We found no French public grant, research tax credit or Bpifrance aid paid to an NVIDIA entity. Its direct public funding in France is the €2.38M it received through EU research projects with French partners.
