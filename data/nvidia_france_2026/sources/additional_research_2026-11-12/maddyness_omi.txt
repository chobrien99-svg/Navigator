With the American giant's chips and technical support, it aims to generate images ten times faster. Temps de lecture : 4 minutes Nvidia continues to strengthen its ties with French Tech. After announcing investments in Doctolib and Pigment last week, the American chip giant is now entering into a technology partnership with Omi, a French startup specializing in creating photos and videos for brands using artificial intelligence. Specifically, the agreement is expected to increase Omi's computing power and accelerate image generation. The startup will notably rely on Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs, the latest generation of professional graphics cards. But the partnership goes beyond simply supplying chips: Nvidia will also provide Omi with technical experts and engineers to help it make the most of its technologies. “With Nvidia's chips, we can serve our customers faster. We will be able to generate images ten times faster. In addition, we will be able to serve twice as many people simultaneously as before,” explains Hugo Borensztein, co-founder of Omi. At the same time, Omi is announcing the launch of ProductDrop, a new AI-powered tool that enables brands to generate highly accurate images of their products at scale. Two Brothers Behind Omi Founded in 2020, Omi was created by two brothers, Hugo and Paul Borensztein. Hugo previously worked at Facebook, while Paul worked at PayFit and Zenly. In March 2020, just before the first lockdown, they decided to embark on their entrepreneurial journey together, focusing on digital twins, virtual 3D replicas of physical objects. The concept? To create a highly accurate digital copy of a product, whether a perfume, a lipstick or a bottle of shampoo. Using this replica, brands can easily generate photorealistic photos and videos for their various communication channels, including e-commerce websites, social media and advertising campaigns. All without requiring any particular technical skills. Six years after its founding, Omi employs around 55 people across Paris and New York. The startup claims to serve more than 1,000 brands, including L'Oréal , Nestlé, Estée Lauder and Shiseido. Its platform now hosts more than 50,000 digital twins and enables the generation of several million visuals each year. Omi Faces Competition from Adobe Despite advances in generative artificial intelligence, traditional photography remains Omi's main competitor, according to Hugo Borensztein. “Brands are going back to it in a big way. They are quite disappointed with generative AI. They are afraid of damaging their image with AI, and in some cases, they are right,” the entrepreneur believes. But competition is also intensifying on the technology front. While specialized players such as Bordeaux-based startup Nfinite are already active in this market, Omi must now contend with the arrival of a heavyweight: Adobe. “Six months ago, Adobe announced the launch of a digital twin platform. They have a strong presence in the United States. We are dealing with a giant. Today, they are our main competitor,” the executive explains. Omi Reports $4 Million in Annual Recurring Revenue in the United States It is across the Atlantic that the French startup is focusing much of its efforts. Having established a presence in New York two years ago, it now employs around ten people there and continues to pursue major American corporate clients. “We reached $4 million in ARR (annual recurring revenue) in the United States in less than 18 months,” reveals Hugo Borensztein. Today, nearly 70% of Omi's revenue is generated outside France. The startup intends to continue expanding, particularly in the American market. Although it does not disclose its overall revenue, Omi says its growth means it does not expect to need another funding round in the immediate future. Since its founding, the company has raised nearly €20 million. Beyond Luxury, a Focus on Everyday Consumer Products Historically focused on the beauty and cosmetics sectors, Omi now intends to expand into the broader consumer goods market. The startup is specifically targeting groups such as Procter & Gamble and Unilever, with which it is already working on certain projects, although they are not yet among its main customers. This diversification should enable the company to continue growing as competition intensifies in the AI-powered visual content creation market.
