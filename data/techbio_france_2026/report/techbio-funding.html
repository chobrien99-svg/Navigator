What the data says

01

Money is extremely concentrated. Owkin (€270M) and DNA Script (€265M) alone account for half the total, all of it raised in 2022 or earlier. The top 10 hold 84%. Below them, the typical company has raised a few million euros.

02

Deal count kept rising after the 2021 peak. 2021 has the most money (€313M) but only 5 rounds. Rounds then climbed every year to 13 in 2024, and 2025 held at 11. The four 2026 rounds so far (Biolevate, WhiteLab Genomics, Rivercell, Generare) already total €90M, at an average of about €22M a round.

03

Series A rounds are getting bigger. The median Series A tranche was €11M up to 2021, about €13M in 2022–23, €20M in 2024 and €24M in 2025–26. Seed rounds peaked at a €5.5M median in 2024. Samples are small (2 to 9 rounds per period), so read this as direction, not precision.

04

Bpifrance is the one constant. The state bank is in 11 of the 34 funded companies and leads 10 rounds. No private investor appears in more than 5. Of 137 distinct investors, 111 back just one company on the list.

05

Generalist tech VCs have taken over the early rounds. Since 2024, 22 of 25 rounds with named investors included a generalist or deeptech VC (HCVC, Alven, Daphni, Kima, Better Angle, Galion.exe). None was backed only by life-sciences specialists, against 3 of 20 rounds up to 2021.

06

Foreign money is common. 20 of the 34 funded companies have at least one foreign investor, most often American (Y Combinator, Khosla, GV, Illumina Ventures, Bristol Myers Squibb) or British (LocalGlobe, Atomico, Entrepreneurs First).

07

It is a Paris-region story. 35 of the 48 companies sit in Île-de-France and hold 97% of the money. The 13 regional companies have raised €26M between them, and 9 of them have no recorded equity round.

08

The cohort is young. 26 of the 48 were registered in 2021 or later, and 10 since 2024. Funded companies took a median of 2 years from registration to their first round. Most are small: only 4 had 50 or more staff in the 2024 registry data.

09

Most of the science comes from public labs, and the patents are concentrated. 28 of the 48 companies have a documented link to French public research, and 25 hold patents. DNA Script and Eligo account for half of the 195 patent families. The AI-first companies mostly have neither: the 12 foundation-model and data companies hold 16 families, 12 of them Owkin’s.

10

Brussels has been a bigger grant-giver than Paris. 15 companies received €31M from EU research programmes, against €1.35M from the French research agency (ANR) as company partners. Five won an EIC Accelerator grant of about €2.5M each: Cherry Biotech, Qubit Pharmaceuticals, Iktos, InSpek and Scienta Lab.

11

Four companies are in court proceedings. Ksilink (September 2026) and Poietis (January 2025) are in judicial liquidation; Poietis's bioprinting business was taken over by a new company, Poietis Biosystems (now NGB Biosystems). Iktos (October 2026) and Ariana Pharma (September 2026) are in receivership, which lets them keep trading while a court-appointed administrator looks for a rescue. Iktos had won a €2.5M EIC Accelerator grant in 2024.

Five kinds of TechBio

The list mixes quite different businesses. Grouping them by what they do shows where investors have put their money. The grouping is ours, based on each company's description.

SegmentCosWith a roundTotal €MMedian €MMedian foundedLargest
  • Foundation models and data platforms are the newest group (median registration 2023) yet have already drawn €435M, led by Owkin (€270M) and Bioptimus (€70M). This is where the big recent cheques go: Bioptimus, Biolevate (€36M) and Rivercell (€21.5M).
  • AI drug and molecule design splits in two. Aqemia has raised €106M in equity (plus a €6.9M France 2030 grant), while 7 of the 12, mostly regional computational chemistry shops such as InSiliBio, AI-biopharma and MAbSilico, have no recorded round.
  • Synthetic biology and genetic medicine has the largest total (€384M) and the highest median (€28M), because these companies need labs and longer development cycles.
  • Precision medicine and lab tech raise small rounds (medians of €2.5M and €5M). Ksilink, a phenotypic drug-discovery lab in Strasbourg, is in judicial liquidation.

Patent families per company

The 25 companies with at least one patent family. A family groups the filings for one invention across countries. The other 23 companies have no patent family in the data.

  • Patents follow the wet lab, not the algorithm. The 8 synthetic-biology and genetic-medicine companies hold 106 families. The 12 foundation-model and data companies hold 16, twelve of them Owkin's. Bioptimus, Rivercell, Biolevate and Cure51 have none. Where the ministry records the type of intellectual property transferred, Aqemia, Orakl and InSiliBio declare trade secrets rather than patents.
  • Filing has picked up. 37 of the 195 families were filed in 2024 or later, mostly by younger companies. Qubit Pharmaceuticals filed 9 of its 16 since October 2024, all with the CNRS and Sorbonne Université, and Netri 5 of its 12. Spore.Bio, Endogene.bio, Orakl and Scienta Lab filed their first patents in 2025–26.
  • Institut Pasteur is the standout institution. It co-owns DNA Script's core enzyme patents (five families, with the CNRS) and Eligo's two founding families, and Ariana Pharma was spun out of it in 2003. Eleven companies have a public lab, university or hospital as a co-owner of at least one patent.
  • Agoranov is the common incubator. 16 of the 30 companies on the official public-research list were incubated at Agoranov, the Paris public-research incubator. Paris Biotech Santé incubated another 5. Eight of the 30 (Brink, DeepLife, DNA Script, Eligo, Iktos, Rivercell, Rosetta Omics, Spore.Bio) are listed only because a public incubator supported them, with no parent lab recorded, so they shouldn't be described as spin-offs of a particular institution.
  • Public research money comes before the private rounds. As academics, founders of 12 companies led or took part in ANR research projects worth about €8.4M to their labs, €5.3M of it before the company existed. Oria's co-founder Abdou Rachid Thiam held 12 ANR grants (€2.4M) at the ENS physics lab, almost as much as Oria's €2.7M seed round. Karavela's Bertrand Thirion held 7 (€1.6M, including a €535k AI chair) at Inria, against a €2M pre-seed. That money funded labs, not the companies.
  • The companies themselves get little direct ANR money. They received about €1.35M as project partners, more than half of it (€0.75M) to Ksilink. Owkin and Qubit Pharmaceuticals are partners in large France 2030 research consortia (Owkin in two hospital projects worth €9–10M each, Qubit in a €36M quantum-computing programme), but their own share isn't published.
  • Twenty companies have no documented public-research link. They're mostly AI- and data-first and recent: Bioptimus, Cure51, Rivercell, Biolevate, Spore.Bio, DeepLife, Opsin and TychoBio among them. "No documented link" doesn't mean no science. Bioptimus co-founder Jean-Philippe Vert, for example, held ANR grants as an academic.
  • Many founders are established researchers. In at least 18 companies, a founder has five or more publications in HAL, the French open archive, under the same lab the company is linked to elsewhere. The longest records belong to Bertrand Thirion (Karavela, Inria, 274 papers), Jean-Philip Piquemal (Qubit, Sorbonne/CNRS, 197), Jean-Philippe Vert (Bioptimus, 137) and Anne Poupon (MAbSilico, CNRS Tours, 109).
  • Only two companies sponsor a registered clinical study. Cure51 leads an observational study of exceptional long-term cancer survivors with Gustave Roussy, Centre Léon Bérard and three other European cancer centres (NCT06160596). Owkin leads a multimodal oncology data-collection study (NCT06625203). Neither tests a treatment.

  • Cherry Biotech is the surprise leader. The small Montreuil organ-on-chip company has taken €8.4M from 18 EU projects: two EIC grants of €2.5M (IDEFIX in 2022, ASTEROID in 2024), two EIC Pathfinder projects, and a long run of Marie Skłodowska-Curie research fellowships and training networks. Its only recorded equity round is a seed round with no disclosed amount.
  • The EIC Accelerator is the main EU door. Qubit (2021), Cherry (2024), Iktos (2024), InSpek (2024) and Scienta Lab (2025) each won about €2.5M in grant money. The EIC can also take equity, which isn't covered here. Eligo had a comparable €2.8M SME Instrument grant in 2019. Ksilink was selected for a 2024 EIC Accelerator but no grant amount is recorded.
  • Owkin's EU money came through big consortia. It took €4.2M as a partner in five Horizon 2020 health and IMI projects, mostly MELLODDY (€2.7M), a federated-learning project with ten drugmakers.
  • Ten companies are approved research providers for the CIR, so clients can claim the research tax credit on work they buy from them: Ksilink, Ariana, Parean, Qubit, MedInsights, AI-Biopharma, Poietis, InSiliBio, Iktos and Netri. InSiliBio and MedInsights are also approved for the innovation tax credit (CII). Approval says a company sells research services, not how much it earns from them.

EU figures are each company’s own share of the EU contribution, from the research ministry’s scanR database (CORDIS data). ANR figures are partner shares from the ANR’s open data. CIR and CII approvals come from the ministry’s published lists of approved bodies.

How reliable is this? Data gaps and method
  • Corrected on 7 November 2026. Aqemia, Bioptimus and Owkin were corrected from press releases and company figures. Owkin's rounds now match its own figures: $2.1M seed, $72M Series A, $230M Series B. $18M of its Series A (GV in 2018, F-Prime and Eight Roads in 2019) is recorded as one estimated entry, because the split was never disclosed. Its Bristol Myers Squibb round counts only the $50M equity part of an $80M deal.
  • Dollar rounds are converted at the approximate euro exchange rate for the month announced, so euro totals can differ slightly from figures in the press.
  • Totals may still be a floor. Some companies may be missing rounds, especially those with no round recorded since mid-2024. WhiteLab Genomics' 2026 Series B (converted from $26M) and Rosetta Omics' pre-seed are estimates. Grants (Aqemia's France 2030 award, Peaccel's regional grant) are excluded from totals.
  • WhiteLab Genomics and Rivercell corrected on 10 November 2026 from editor-supplied funding tables. WhiteLab: a €150k angel round (2020) is added and its September 2022 round is a $10M Series A, not a seed; a €150k WILCO loan, a €300k Bpifrance convertible and two small grants are recorded but left out of the equity totals. Rivercell's seed is €22.7M, no longer an estimate.
  • Qubit Pharmaceuticals corrected on 10 November 2026: a Quantonation pre-seed (mid-2020, undisclosed) and a €5M EIC Fund equity investment (June 2021) are added, and the European Innovation Council is no longer shown as an investor in its 2022 seed round, which press coverage attributes to XAnge, Omnes Capital, Quantonation and Octave Klaba.
  • Iktos corrected on 10 November 2026 from an editor-supplied funding history: its 2023 Series A is €15.5M, and a €0.9M Bpifrance loan (2021) is recorded as debt and left out of the equity totals.
  • Eligo Bioscience updated on 10 November 2026: its 2023 Series B was extended from $30M to $35M (announced May 2025; the extra $5M is recorded as an estimated €4.4M). Eligo's 2017 Khosla round is undisclosed. Its April 2018 €1.8M entry may be grant funding rather than equity; it stays in the total but is unconfirmed. Grants (CARB-X $7.1M in 2020, a France 2030 award of $5M in 2025, and a $4.5M grant in 2019) are recorded separately.
  • Rounds stored as €0 were treated as undisclosed. Investor names were merged where the same fund appears under two spellings (e.g. Sofinnova / Sofinnova Partners).
  • Founding year is the registration date of the French legal entity from the national registry (recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr), which can be later than when the team started. All 48 companies now have a SIREN in the database.
  • Founders are recorded for all 48 companies. Tebuna's three founding shareholders (Millie Heo, its president, with 95.7%, plus Cyril Touffet and Michel Popielarz) come from its founding statutes. Founder records were cleaned on 10 November 2026: names stored together in one record (Bioptimus, Spore.Bio, Netri) were split, and duplicates removed.
  • Headcount bands are the registry's 2024 figures for the French entity only.
  • Patents come from the research ministry's patent-applicant dataset, completed on 7–8 November 2026 by a full search of INPI's patent database (French, European and international publications) for every company, checked by SIREN or exact applicant name with a French address. That added 41 families, mostly 2024–26 filings, and real titles for all but 10 families. Look-alikes (Nebula Inc, Endogene Ltd, Opsin Biotherapeutics Inc) were rejected; Owkin Inc, the US parent, is counted as Owkin. Applications publish about 18 months after filing, so the newest filings may still be missing.
  • EU grants are each company's own share in Horizon 2020 and Horizon Europe projects, from scanR (CORDIS data). Projects where the share is zero or unrecorded are left out. EIC equity investments are not in the grant figures; Qubit's €5M EIC Fund investment is counted in its equity total.
  • Court proceedings come from BODACC notices matched on SIREN. A first name-based search returned false matches (a 2019 notice and an unrelated restaurant), which were discarded.
  • Publications are counted from HAL only where the founder's affiliations match the lab already linked to the company. Common names (e.g. Eric Durand) were not counted.
  • The ANR grant search covered every founder and every company, in the ANR's generic-call data (2005–09 and 2010 onwards) and its France 2030 programme data. Amounts are the partner's share of each project. Remaining limits: 2005–09 records carry no researcher IDs, so those matches rest on first name and institution (Jean-Philippe Vert, Bertrand Thirion, Jean-Philip Piquemal); France 2030 programme data gives only consortium totals, so Owkin's and Qubit's own shares are unknown; and a company that took part under another legal name would be missed.
  • The official public-research start-up list matched DNA Script only under an older, closed company number. Rivercell appears under its former legal name, Blossom Life Sciences.
