# Top-Heavy: French Tech's Q2 Rebound Rested on a Single Deal

Here's a number to start with: **€2.09 billion**. That's what French tech startups raised in Q2 2026 — April through June — according to our [French Tech Funding Tracker](https://funding.frenchtechjournal.com). It's up 16% on the same quarter last year, with four rounds above €100 million. On paper, the long-awaited rebound has arrived.

Now here's the number that complicates it: **€480 million**. That's Alan, the Paris health-insurance unicorn, in a single June round — nearly a quarter of everything raised in the entire quarter. Take that one deal out, and French tech funding didn't rise at all. It *fell* about 11%.

That gap — between an encouraging headline and a market that's actually thinning beneath it — is the story of Q2 2026. (It wasn't even 2026's biggest quarter: Q1 raised 23% more.) We're calling it **Top-Heavy**.

## The whole gain was one company

Let's be precise about this, because it's the most important sentence in the report. With Alan, Q2 2026 was up 15.7% year-on-year. Without Alan, it was down 10.8%. The entire year-on-year *gain* is a single deal — at 22.9% of the quarter, the highest share any one round has taken in six years of our data. (For comparison: Mistral's big 2024 round was 20.3% of its quarter; last year's biggest deal, Knave, was just 5.5%.)

This isn't a market in distress. The median round actually *rose*, from €3.5M to €5.0M — a six-year high — and late-stage capital is plentiful. But it is a market that has stopped widening.

## Zoom out, and 2026 genuinely looks strong — with an asterisk

Widen the lens to the whole first half and the picture brightens. By June, 2026 had raised **€4.83 billion** — already 61% of *all* of 2025, in half the time, and up 46% on H1 2025. On any reasonable run-rate, 2026 clears both 2025 and 2024 to become France's strongest year since the 2022 boom.

The asterisk, again, is concentration. Two deals — AMI's €890M in Q1 and Alan's €480M in Q2 — are 28% of the half-year and about **91% of the entire year-on-year gain**. Strip both out and H1 2026 is essentially flat on 2025, on the fewest H1 deals in six years. Bigger headline, thinner base.

## June did the heavy lifting

Inside the quarter, the shape was lopsided. April was weak — €305M, roughly half of April 2025. Then June carried everything: **€1.30 billion, 62% of the quarter**, as Alan, Morpho Labs (€151M) and Bionyra Pharma (€140M) all landed in the same four weeks. Even stripping Alan, June was still the strongest month.

## The money left AI for medicine

The sector story is a rotation. After two years as French tech's growth engine, **AI funding more than halved** — €513M to €234M, with deal count down 42%. (You'll see headlines that AI *surged* over the half-year; that's entirely AMI's €890M Q1 round. Strip it out and AI is down at both the quarter and half-year level. The wave crested in Q2 2024 and has receded two years running.)

Where did the capital go? Into **health and biotech**, the quarter's genuine, broad-based winner — up 57% year-on-year, led by Bionyra's €140M Series A, one of the largest French biotech A-rounds on record, with Sanofi Ventures, Jeito and Sofinnova writing big cheques. And into **deeptech and quantum**: Grenoble startup Quobly raised €115M, part of a €238M quarter that made Grenoble the country's No. 2 hub after Paris.

## The thinning base

Here's the structural worry. Seed and pre-seed rounds fell from 91 to 57 deals year-on-year — and from a 2022 peak of 196 to just 71, a **64% collapse in early-stage deal-making**. Meanwhile Series A cheques grew 80% and growth-stage funding hit a near-six-year high. The top of the market is swelling while the funnel that's supposed to refill it contracts. That's what "top-heavy" means structurally, not just rhetorically.

One actor is holding the line: **Bpifrance**, the public investment bank, touched 22 of the quarter's 139 deals — more than four times any private fund — and is most present in exactly the early stage private money is abandoning. The state is increasingly the floor under French seed.

## What we're watching in H2

Three questions decide how 2026 is remembered. Does the deal count finally stop falling — or is the thinning structural? Is Alan a one-off, or a signal that late-stage capital is genuinely reopening at home? And will the full year clear €9 billion — and if it does, how much of that will be a handful of giants rather than a broad, healthy market?

French tech will almost certainly raise more in 2026 than it did in 2025. Whether that means the market is healthier, or just that a few companies got very big, is the question the second half will answer.
